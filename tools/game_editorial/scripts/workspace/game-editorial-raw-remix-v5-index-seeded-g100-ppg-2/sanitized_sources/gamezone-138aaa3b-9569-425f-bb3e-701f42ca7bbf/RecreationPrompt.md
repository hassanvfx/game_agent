Create a browser game called **Brainrot Fusion** as a polished single-screen, portrait-friendly arcade merge puzzler with a loud meme-chaos personality. The game should play like a Suika-style physics stacker: the player controls a horizontal drop lane across the top of a full-screen playfield, moves it with the mouse on desktop or by dragging a finger on mobile, and taps/clicks to release the current orb into the pit below. Every dropped object should behave like a glossy bouncy sphere with gravity, friction, floor bounce, wall collisions, and object-on-object stacking, so board shape and momentum matter.

Use a seven-tier merge ladder built around absurd meme-coded orb characters. Start with small low-tier pieces such as a skull, clown, and coin, then let matching pairs merge into progressively larger forms like a moai head, alien, brain, and finally a cosmic end-tier orb. Only the first few smallest tiers should spawn directly; all larger pieces must be created through merges. When two orbs of the same tier collide, remove both and spawn a new higher-tier orb at their midpoint with a little upward pop. Award score for every merge, with bigger merges worth more. Show the current score clearly in the HUD and always preview the next piece.

The core gameplay loop should be immediate and endless: aim, drop, watch the physics settle, set up the next merge, rescue bad stacks, and chase a higher score. Add a visible warning line near the top of the arena. If any settled portion of the pile remains inside that danger zone for long enough, trigger a game-over state. After losing, show the final score and offer a quick restart so runs feel fast and replayable rather than level-based.

Presentation should feel flashy and arcade-juicy. Use a neon purple, cyan, pink, and gold palette with a dark futuristic background. Include a bold stylized title screen, a compact HUD for score and next orb, and a dramatic game-over card. Render the game on canvas, but keep menu and HUD overlays in HTML/CSS. Add floating slang or “brainrot” words drifting in the background for personality. The tone should be silly, exaggerated, and self-aware rather than serious.

Audio and feedback are important signature elements. Include looping background music plus distinct sound effects for starting the run, dropping a piece, hard collisions, successful merges, next-piece updates, and failure. Add funny reaction media or animated visual callouts for big merges and for losing. Overall, the recreation should feel like a meme-skinned physics merge game with simple controls, readable UI, strong audiovisual payoff, and short score-chasing sessions optimized for both desktop and mobile play.
