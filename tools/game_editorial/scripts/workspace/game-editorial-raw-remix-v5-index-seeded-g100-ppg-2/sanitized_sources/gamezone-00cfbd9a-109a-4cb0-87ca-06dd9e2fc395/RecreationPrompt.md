Create a compact, replayable interactive romance game called "Sunday Sketch" for mobile and desktop browsers. Present it as a dreamy visual-novel-style vignette set in a peaceful park during a quiet Sunday afternoon. The player experiences the scene as Mia, an artist sketching outdoors, when a stranger named Leo approaches and asks whether the bench is free. Build the game around a simple loop: begin from a stylish title screen, tap or click to advance typewriter dialogue, make occasional dialogue choices, branch into different emotional outcomes, then replay from the ending screen.

Use a full-screen scenic background with softly lit, storybook-like colors and stage two character sprites on a canvas layer so they can fade and slide into position as the conversation evolves. Overlay the canvas with polished glassmorphism UI: a floating title chip, a mute button, a bottom dialogue panel, a speaker name tag, a circular portrait that updates with the current speaker, and a subtle continue indicator when text is ready to advance. Include gentle background music, light UI tap sounds, and distinct positive or awkward feedback cues when choices are made. Short animated reaction stickers or GIF-like overlays are appropriate as long as they reinforce the emotional tone without blocking the main interaction.

The story should be short and highly readable. Start with narration that frames the mood of the park and invites the player to decide how open Mia wants to be. Leo should introduce himself as someone who likes sketching people and moments in the park. The first major choice should be whether Mia lets him sit and share the bench or keeps to herself. If she declines, the story should end in a solitary but calm ending. If she accepts, continue into a gentle conversation about sketching and observation. Then present a second choice: Mia can either ask Leo to compare notes and deepen the connection or politely thank him and return to her own work. These branches should lead to three endings overall: a warm shared-sketchbook ending, a neutral quiet-study ending, and a private-afternoon ending.

Keep the pacing intimate and low-pressure. There should be no combat, scoring, timers, inventory, or exploration systems. The core appeal is emotional branching through dialogue, supported by attractive presentation, smooth transitions, and a tender, reflective, slightly wistful mood. Make the final end cards summarize the emotional result in a short title and description, and include a clear replay button so players can see every branch.
