Recreate **SCRAP YARD '95'** as a polished fullscreen browser game that plays like a tiny retro arcade destruction sandbox set in a neon junkyard. Present it as a single-screen HTML5 canvas experience with a stylish start overlay, bold arcade title treatment, CRT scanlines, glowing rails, hazard-strip accents, and a moody industrial color palette of deep blue steel, cyan glow, orange sparks, and warm rust tones. The overall vibe should feel like a 1990s scrapyard broadcast or “wreck lot simulator” with playful attitude rather than realism.

The core gameplay loop should be immediate and tactile: the player presses a start button to open the lot, taps or clicks a **spawn scrap** button to drop random junk into the arena, then directly grabs any item with mouse or touch and drags it around the field. When released, objects should keep some light momentum and slide in a top-down space with friction and soft wall bounces. The player’s main goal is to create satisfying collisions by slamming one object into another. When two objects overlap closely enough, both should be destroyed instantly with punchy audiovisual feedback and a burst of particles. The play loop is: spawn junk, drag junk, crash junk, enjoy the explosion, repeat. Include a separate **clear lot** button that detonates every remaining object in one dramatic reset and returns the field to an empty sandbox state.

Use a small but memorable catalog of scrap items with distinct silhouettes and funny personality, such as a rusty door, a yellow bus, a wooden log, an orange ball, a milk carton, and a pencil. Each item should have unique dimensions and handcrafted visual details so they are easy to recognize at a glance. Lean into slightly cartoonish presentation, including expressive markings or face-like details on some junk pieces, so the destruction feels comedic rather than violent. Make the milk carton a special-case collision target that bursts into brighter white splash particles and uses a softer impact flavor than the normal fiery debris used for the other objects.

Build the interaction model for both desktop and mobile. Mouse and touch should both support pick-up, drag, move, and release. The HUD should stay out of the way while remaining thumb-friendly on phones, with safe-area-aware spacing and large arcade buttons. Show a compact live counter that displays how many active wrecks or objects are currently in the lot. Add short animated feedback callouts for notable moments, such as a crowded yard, a strong collision chain, or a reset event, using flashy badge-style overlays that appear briefly and then fade.

Audio is important to the feel: include a looping arcade-like background track plus responsive sounds for start, spawn, drag, impact, milk impact, and reset. Impacts should feel crunchy and exaggerated rather than realistic. Visually, collisions should produce particle bursts, glow, and quick transient excitement that make repeated play satisfying even without structured progression.

Do not turn this into a level game, puzzle campaign, or score chase. Keep it open-ended, toy-like, and replayable, with no fail state and no formal ending. The signature mechanic is direct manipulation of spawned scrapyard objects inside a single arena, where the fun comes from low-friction interaction, repeated crash payoff, and maintaining a loud, goofy, chaotic wreck-lot mood.
