Create a single-screen browser game called "Orbital Cleanup Command" that plays like a futuristic city-destruction arcade sandbox on desktop and mobile. Show a full-screen top-down/isometric-feeling miniature city rendered on an HTML5 canvas, with roads, moving traffic, dense buildings, occasional rivers or park zones, and randomized district layouts such as grid, sprawl, sector, river, and park. The player should act as an orbital commander and use a HUD to pick one of six strike tools—an inexpensive kinetic pulse drop, a cluster burst bombardment, a sustained beam laser, a vacuum-like singularity, a meteor storm, and a summoned roaming destruction bot/kaiju—then tap or click anywhere on the city to deploy that tool at the target point. Add an energy meter that regenerates over time and makes stronger tools cost more, with an optional toggle that disables limits for sandbox play. Award score for destroying buildings and vehicles, and make the fun come from chaining large destructive hits across dense urban areas. Include dynamic conditions that alter play: randomized weather like clear, rain, acid rain, snow, and storm; a day/night toggle that changes both palette and background music; and a time-scale toggle for slow, normal, or fast simulation. Build in signature interactions, such as rain or storms letting the laser chain lightning between nearby structures, river areas causing laser steam effects, weather suppressing or enabling fire spread from explosive attacks, the singularity pulling in traffic and even consuming other projectiles to become stronger, and the summoned bot becoming enraged if hit by the laser. Present everything with a flashy neon command-center UI: glowing glass panels, bold sci-fi typography, score readout, district and weather labels, animated feedback banners, combo text, screen shake, particles, smoke, lightning flashes, haptics where available, and short celebratory or warning reaction media outside the core playfield. Keep the overall tone playful and spectacular—framed as "cleanup" but obviously about stylish urban devastation—and make the game feel like an endlessly replayable score-chasing sandbox rather than a story campaign.
