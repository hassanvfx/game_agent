Create a polished browser game called **Moon Run**, a neon sci-fi one-button endless survival arcade game inspired by the feel of Flappy Bird but framed as a lunar mission. The player controls a glowing beacon-like spacecraft or mission core that stays around the left side of the screen while the world scrolls from right to left. The entire game should be readable instantly, playable in very short sessions, and feel tuned for “one more try” score chasing.

Use a **single full-screen canvas** for gameplay and layer simple HTML UI on top for the start screen, the in-game HUD, and the game-over screen. The start screen should introduce the mission tone with a title like **MOON RUN** and a subtitle that explains the core loop: tap or press Space to keep the beacon steady and thread through hazard bands. The HUD during play should be minimal and prominent, showing the current score as **ALTITUDE** in meters. The game-over screen should clearly announce failure, display the peak altitude reached, and offer a fast retry button.

The control scheme must be **one-button only**: tapping, clicking, or pressing Space instantly applies a strong upward thrust, while gravity constantly drags the player downward. This should create a rhythmic flap-like movement pattern where the player continuously makes tiny corrections to stay centered. The run begins only when the player taps a launch button, and after that every touch or press acts as thrust. The game should work well on both desktop and mobile, with touch-safe controls and a layout that respects small screens.

The gameplay loop is simple and strict: survive, steer through gaps, score higher, repeat. Spawn obstacle pairs from the right side of the screen as vertical hazard bands made of a top wall and a bottom wall with a randomized gap between them. Move them left across the screen at a steady pace that gradually increases over time. If the player collides with the ceiling, the floor, or any solid part of an obstacle, the run ends immediately. Award continuous score over time to represent altitude gained, and add a larger score bonus every time the player successfully clears an obstacle pair. As difficulty progression, slowly increase scroll speed so timing becomes tighter the longer the run lasts. Keep progression endless rather than level-based.

Presentation should lean hard into a **retro-futurist lunar aesthetic**. Use a deep-space background with dark navy-to-black gradients, cyan and violet neon accents, a subtle glowing moon or radial light bloom, and a moving sci-fi grid to imply speed and depth. The player object should look like a bright, polished mission core or orb-like shuttle with a luminous center, a slight cockpit detail, and a small exhaust flare or energy tail. Rotate the player slightly based on vertical velocity so it tips upward when thrusting and downward when falling. Leave a glowing trail behind the player that visualizes recent flight history and enhances the sense of motion.

Add arcade juice and reactive feedback throughout the run. Each jump should feel sharp and responsive. When the player clears an obstacle, show floating text particles such as mission-control style status words like “CLEAR,” “BOOST,” or “ON COURSE.” Add small celebratory feedback cards or banners for launch, big score moments, and crashes. Include mild screen shake on thrust or impact. Audio should be simple but effective: a launch sound, a jump sound, a score chime, a crash sound, and a looping background music track that starts with the run and stops on game over.

Keep the tone energetic, clean, and slightly heroic rather than comedic or narrative-heavy. This should feel like a highly readable, mobile-friendly arcade score attack game with neon-space polish, fast restarts, and a strong survival focus built around gravity control and threading through hazard gaps.
