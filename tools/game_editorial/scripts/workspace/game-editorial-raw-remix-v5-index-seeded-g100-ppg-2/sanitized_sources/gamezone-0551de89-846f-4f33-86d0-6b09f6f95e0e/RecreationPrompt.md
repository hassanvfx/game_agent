Create a polished mobile-friendly browser arcade game called **Relay Launch** set in a neon futuristic sprint arena. Present it full-screen with a dark blue sci-fi sky, subtle starfield, glowing skyline silhouettes, luminous lane markings, and clean holographic UI styling. The player should see a runner standing on a launch platform near the left side of the screen with a small relay object, coin-like token, or energy baton positioned to be launched forward.

The core gameplay loop must be a **three-tap timing challenge**. On the first tap, begin the attempt and switch from an idle prompt into a power-selection phase. During this phase, an on-screen power meter should automatically sweep back and forth from low to high, with a clearly highlighted perfect zone that rewards ideal timing. On the second tap, lock the current power and move into an angle-selection phase. During this phase, display a curved aiming arc near the runner and sweep the launch angle automatically between a low and high bound. On the third tap, lock the angle and immediately launch the relay object.

After launch, the game should become a **physics-driven distance run**. Simulate horizontal and vertical velocity, gravity, bouncing on the ground, reduced bounce at lower speeds, and rolling friction until the object eventually stops. The camera should track the object horizontally so long travel distances feel dramatic and readable. Show the current distance traveled in meters during the flight.

Build the course around a **checkpoint collision system**. Place visible checkpoint pillars every 100 meters, and make every 200-meter marker a special launcher checkpoint that boosts and redirects the projectile when hit. Normal checkpoints should behave like obstacles or bumpers, while launcher checkpoints should create more dramatic rebounds and momentum gains. The player should be able to get longer runs through both skillful timing and fortunate interactions with these course elements.

Use rich arcade feedback throughout: satisfying sounds for starting, calibrating, launching, bouncing, hitting milestones, receiving boosts, and finishing a run; brief impact feedback during the launch sequence; and celebratory visual feedback when the attempt ends. Include a sleek result overlay once the object has fully stopped. That overlay should prominently show the final distance, the number of checkpoints cleared, and a single strong call-to-action button to immediately try again.

The tone should feel **fast, flashy, readable, and replayable**. The entire design should support quick repeated score-chasing attempts on both desktop and touch devices, with simple controls, snappy restarts, and a presentation that feels like a futuristic sports-arcade mini-game rather than a realistic simulation.
