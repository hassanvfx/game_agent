Create a polished browser game called **Signal Truce** as a one-screen, mobile-friendly real-time territory conquest experience. The playfield should be a dark, glowing, cyber-themed map of connected polygon regions shaped like a stylized Europe, with every region acting as a command node in a hostile signal network. Use a full-screen canvas for the map and floating futuristic HUD panels for menus, status, speed controls, pause, and a special ability button.

Start with a pre-game selection phase where the player taps or clicks any region to choose their opening command hub. Once the match starts, every region should belong to a different rival faction, and each faction's starting region should count as a capital. Owned regions should automatically generate troop strength over time up to a cap. The player interacts by dragging from one owned region to an adjacent connected region to send **half** of that region's current troops as a moving packet along the link. Friendly arrivals reinforce; hostile arrivals reduce enemy troop counts until the region flips ownership.

Make capital capture the signature snowball mechanic: when a capital is conquered, that entire faction should be annexed instantly, converting all of its remaining regions and any in-flight troops to the winning side. Add a visible devastation or damage system so heavily contested regions feel worn down and have reduced capacity. Enemy AI factions should periodically inspect neighboring regions and launch attacks or reinforcements on their own, creating steady multi-front pressure.

Include a defensive special ability named **Signal Burst** or **Jammer** that temporarily prevents enemy attacks from targeting player-controlled regions, then enters a cooldown. Also include speed cycling (1x, 2x, 4x, and 0.5x), pause/resume, music toggles, and game-over screens for victory or defeat. Present troop numbers directly over regions, highlight capitals, show moving unit packets traveling between nodes, and add flashy feedback such as impact animations, annexation notifications, and a celebratory victory badge.

The tone should feel like a compact cyber-RTS: easy to read, fast to understand, dramatic in momentum swings, and suitable for touch or mouse play without requiring keyboard controls. The goal is to capture the feel of a streamlined graph-conquest strategy game where smart expansion paths, defense timing, and capital snipes determine who controls the entire network.
