Create a mobile-first top-down management game called **Arcade Snack Rush: Crew Drill** set inside a neon-lit arcade snack venue with three horizontal wings. The player controls a single worker directly with a virtual joystick and one large context-sensitive action button, so the whole game should feel easy to play on a phone with touch controls and short, readable UI overlays.

Start the player in the center wing, which contains the core service stations: a grill for cooking, a sink for washing trays, a trash can for discarding held food, a front counter where customers queue, and a couple of tables for seating. The left and right wings should exist from the beginning as visible adjacent spaces, but they start locked and can later be unlocked with money. The player can walk between wings by exiting the screen horizontally, making the venue feel like one continuous business expanded across rooms.

Build the main gameplay loop around fast, real-time snack service. Customers should appear over time, approach the counter, and display one of several food orders such as burger, pizza, salad, or sushi. When the player serves the counter interaction, customers should be assigned to an available table in an owned wing and begin waiting with a visible patience timer. The player must then cook the correct item, pick it up, walk it to the right table, and serve it before the customer loses patience. After eating, the customer leaves behind cash at the table that can be collected. If the wrong food is delivered, the game should clearly reject it. If a customer waits too long, they should leave angrily and the opportunity is lost.

Include light but important resource management. Cooking should consume both ingredient supplies and a clean tray, so the player must keep supplies stocked and periodically wash trays to restore dish capacity. Let the player buy more ingredients from an upgrade hub menu. If the player is holding unwanted food, they can throw it away in the trash. Add a steady economic pressure by charging payroll every minute for hired staff, so the player is pushed to keep revenue flowing.

Progression should move from manual multitasking into semi-automated business management. Provide recruitable staff roles with clear specialties: a cashier who auto-takes queued orders, a chef who auto-cooks pending tickets, a waiter who auto-collects money from completed tables, and a washer who auto-refills trays when dish stock runs low. Staff should exist physically in the world, and the player should be able to stand near a staff member and trigger a timed training drill that costs money and levels that worker up, increasing their speed or efficiency. This training action should feel like an active upgrade rather than a passive menu stat bump.

Add business expansion systems. The player should be able to purchase more tables in owned wings and unlock the side wings for a large one-time cost, increasing total seating capacity and long-term throughput. The pace should encourage the player to stabilize the central room first, then scale outward into a larger snack empire.

Present the game with a clean canvas-based playfield and flashy arcade-themed UI. Use bold stylized title fonts, a readable HUD for money, trays, and ingredients, a visible action progress bar for cooking and washing, upgrade and recipe selection modals, floating text feedback for gains and mistakes, and celebratory or warning visual reactions for success and failure. The tone should be colorful, energetic, and slightly hectic, like a casual arcade management game rather than a serious simulator. Keep the mechanics readable, the controls simple, and the overall experience focused on satisfying service flow, crew upgrades, and venue expansion.
