Recreate a polished mobile-friendly HTML5 canvas arcade game called **Night Line Dash**. The game should feel like a sleek neon endless runner set on futuristic midnight railway tracks, viewed from a top-down or slightly angled 2D perspective. The player controls a bright, glowing yellow rail-runner vehicle positioned near the bottom of the screen and must survive by dodging large incoming trains that rush down the track from above.

The game loop should be extremely simple and readable: start a run, react to incoming trains, switch lanes to avoid collisions, score points for every obstacle successfully cleared, watch the challenge intensify as speed increases, then crash, see the final score, and restart instantly for another attempt. Use **three fixed vertical lanes** across the screen. The player cannot move freely; they only swap between left, center, and right lanes. Controls should be touch-first and also work with mouse input: tapping or clicking the **left half** of the screen moves the player one lane left, and tapping or clicking the **right half** moves one lane right. A tap on the start overlay begins play, and a restart button on the game-over screen starts a new run.

Presentation should strongly sell the game’s identity. Use a **neon sci-fi night aesthetic** with glowing rails, dark blue and black backgrounds, cyan highlights, warm yellow-orange player accents, and red-pink danger colors for enemy trains. Add a stylized city skyline in the background, glassy HUD panels, futuristic headline typography, and subtle animated polish like pulsing prompts, glow, tilt on lane changes, particles, and screen shake. The start screen should show the title “Night Line Dash,” a short line about dodging incoming trains, and a “tap to start” cue. During gameplay, show a compact HUD with a score labeled as cleared obstacles. After collision, trigger a dramatic game-over sequence with crash feedback, particles, shake, a short delay, and a game-over overlay that displays the final score plus a strong restart call-to-action such as “Run Again.”

Important systems to preserve: endless obstacle spawning in random lanes, basic anti-overlap spawning logic so lanes are not unfairly blocked at the same moment, smooth interpolation toward the target lane rather than instant teleporting, collision detection against long train obstacles, score gain when a train safely exits the screen, and continuous difficulty escalation through gradually increasing speed and spawn pressure. Add responsive feedback for lane shifts, clean dodges, and crashes using sound effects, short status text, or reaction visuals if desired. Keep the experience focused on **fast reflexes, clean readability, replayability, and mobile-safe one-touch lane dodging**, not on story, inventory, upgrades, or complex level structure.
