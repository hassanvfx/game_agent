Create a polished mobile-first top-down neon arena survival shooter called "Guardian Arena: Trinity." The player begins on a stylish sci-fi selection screen and chooses one of three guardian shells: Fury, a high-damage explosive attacker with slow cooldown; Winter, a fast-firing low-damage shell that slows enemies; and Lighting, a medium-cooldown lightning shell whose attacks chain between nearby enemies. After selection, start a score-based survival run inside a full-screen abstract cyber arena rendered on a canvas with glowing gradients, a faint grid, energetic particles, and a sleek HUD showing score and wave. Control the character entirely with pointer input: dragging acts like a virtual joystick for movement, while a quick tap fires a projectile toward the tapped point. Enemies should spawn continuously from the edges of the screen, move directly toward the player, scale upward in speed and health as score increases, and instantly end the run if they touch the player. Each projectile should support class-specific behavior: explosive area damage for Fury, slowing hits for Winter, and chain lightning jumps for Lighting. Add strong arcade feedback with glow effects, particle bursts, cooldown rings, hit and defeat popups, start and game-over overlays, and energetic combat audio. The progression should be endless survival rather than level completion, with score increasing per kill, wave count rising at score milestones, and the whole experience feeling fast, readable, replayable, and optimized for touch screens.
