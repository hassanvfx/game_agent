Create a polished mobile-first top-down action boss duel called "Protocol: Black Knight" set in a dark neon techno-city containment zone. The experience should begin with a striking title screen and concise instructions, then transition into a fullscreen top-down arena where the player controls a glowing blue cyber-operative moving through a sparse scrolling district with scattered building obstacles and a few ambient civilians. Use touch-first controls: a virtual joystick on the left for movement and large action buttons on the right for attack and special actions. Show a clean HUD with player HP at the top and keep the presentation sleek, glassy, and high-contrast with sci-fi fonts, moody gradients, and strong combat readability.

Structure the run as a short setup phase followed by a single deliberate boss encounter. During the initial roaming phase, let the player move freely in the district while atmospheric background music plays. Include a prominent "Breach" button that the player presses when ready; this should trigger a dark portal or fountain-like summoning effect near the player, screen shake, more intense combat music, disappearance of the civilians, activation of a boss HP bar, and the arrival of the Black Knight.

Make the Black Knight the centerpiece of the game. The boss should feel like a heavy, ominous, readable enemy in a one-on-one duel. Its behavior loop should include chasing the player, performing a close-range slash with a visible windup when near, and launching a spinning projectile when the player is farther away. The player should attack with short-range melee swings that only deal damage in close proximity, encouraging risky positioning. The signature mechanic must be a timed parry on a cooldown: the player can activate parry during a brief defensive window to block an incoming slash or projectile, avoid damage, trigger bright cyan defensive feedback, and stun the boss long enough to create a safe punish window. If the parry is mistimed, the player should take significant damage, be knocked back, and receive dramatic hit feedback.

Emphasize arcade juice and clarity throughout the fight. Use particles, floating damage numbers, flashes, shake, and bold sound cues for attacks, parries, hits, summoning, defeat, and victory. Keep the combat loop simple but skill-based: move, bait an attack, parry at the right moment, punish the stun, then reposition and repeat. End the run with either a defeat screen if player HP reaches zero or a victory screen if the boss is fully depleted, and allow immediate replay from the start of the run. The overall tone should feel tense, stylish, compact, and replayable, like a self-contained boss-fight microgame built for mobile play.
