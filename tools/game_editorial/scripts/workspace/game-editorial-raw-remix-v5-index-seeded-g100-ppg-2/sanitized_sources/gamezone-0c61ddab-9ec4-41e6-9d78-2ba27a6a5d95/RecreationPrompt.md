Recreate **Meme Chaos Lab** as a polished mobile-first browser sandbox game focused on painting reactive materials into a chunky pixel simulation chamber. The whole game should live on one screen with a full-window canvas, a glowing neon sci-fi interface, and a meme-inflected “chaos lab” presentation: dark blue cosmic background, cyan/teal/violet highlights, glassy HUD chips, a dramatic title screen, and a pause screen that summarizes the current chaos count. The player should begin on a start overlay, tap a large start button, then interact directly with the simulation by tapping or dragging on the canvas.

The core gameplay loop is freeform experimentation. The player chooses a material from a bottom palette and selects one of three brush sizes, then paints into the chamber to create chain reactions and interesting motion. Include seven material/tool options: an erase/reset tool, a sand-like material that falls and piles, a water-like material that flows down and sideways, an immovable stone block for barriers, a static plant-like material that can burn, a fire material that rises, spreads to burnable neighbors, and disappears over time, and an acid-like liquid that flows and occasionally dissolves non-stone matter it touches. Preserve the feel of a falling-sand toy rather than a level-based puzzle game.

Use a low-resolution simulation grid scaled up to fill the screen so the pixels look large and crisp. Sand should be able to fall through empty space and settle diagonally when blocked. Liquids should move downward first and spread horizontally when they cannot fall. Fire should ignite adjacent plant matter, be extinguished by water, and naturally burn out. Acid should act like a liquid but also corrode some neighboring materials. Track the number of active particles and show it in a top HUD as the current “Chaos” count.

Support touch and mouse/pointer input cleanly, with thumb-safe controls and no need for keyboard input. Add a pause/menu button in the top corner that opens an overlay with options to resume or purge the board completely. When the player paints a large enough area at once, trigger a combo-like celebratory feedback moment with a sound and a floating reaction image or similar meme-style visual flourish. Also add distinct sounds for starting the game, selecting tools, painting, blocked placement against stone, pausing, resuming, and clearing the chamber, plus a looping ambient arcade background track.

Do not turn it into a story game or score chase. The experience should remain an expressive, satisfying sandbox toy where the pleasure comes from remixing materials, creating barriers and cascades, watching systems interact, and repeatedly resetting the chamber to build fresh chaotic setups.
